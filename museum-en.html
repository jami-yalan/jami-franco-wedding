Founding Exhibition

Yalán-Salazar Family

Permanent Collection · Nine Works

This exhibition brings together nine works from the permanent collection of Jamilet Yalán and Franco Salazar. The objects gathered here were not produced with museographic intent: they are everyday artefacts, photographic records, self-produced works, and documents of a story that began in a faculty of art in 2011 and that tonight reaches one of its most publicly visible moments. The collection is, by nature, permanent and perpetually unfinished.

I Magic: The Gathering Decks
MTG Deck · Vorel
[1]

Commander Deck: Vorel of the Hull Clade

Jamilet Yalán c. 2012 – present 1 commander card, 4 selected cards Magic: The Gathering · Commander Format Yalán-Salazar Collection

Originally constructed from a Simic guild deck from the "Return to Ravnica" expansion during its owner's university years at the Faculty of Art, this deck has been continuously refined over more than a decade of active play. The choice of Vorel of the Hull Clade as commander reflects a sustained preference for proliferation mechanics and adaptive growth — characteristics that, in retrospect, might be read as biographical.

MTG Deck · Kykar
[2]

Commander Deck: Kykar, Wind's Fury

Franco Salazar c. 2012 – present 1 commander card, 4 selected cards Magic: The Gathering · Commander Format Yalán-Salazar Collection

Originating from an old bird deck that came into Franco's hands in 2012 and was later transformed into an Azorius deck during "Return to Ravnica," this deck was subsequently restructured around Kykar, Wind's Fury — a Jeskai commander whose blue-red-white alignment represents a deliberate expansion beyond its origins. The deck functions as a living document of its builder's shifting aesthetic within the game.

II Family Portraits
Portrait · Jamilet
[3]

Portrait of Jamilet Yalán (unfinished)

Franco Salazar 14 February 2025 Acrylic on canvas Yalán-Salazar Collection

Executed on Valentine's Day 2025 as part of a simultaneous portrait exchange, this work depicts the co-founder of the collection in bust against a turquoise background. Salazar's treatment — characterised by simplified colour planes and an economy of line that nonetheless achieves a recognisable likeness — places the work within an expressionist tradition of intimate portraiture. The piece was incorporated into the collection in an unfinished state, a condition the artist has not ruled out resolving at some undetermined date.

Portrait · Franco
[4]

Portrait of Franco Salazar

Jamilet Yalán 14 February 2025 Acrylic on canvas Yalán-Salazar Collection

Produced in the same session as "Portrait of Jamilet Yalán," this work adopts a markedly different approach: a palette of bluish greys, greater attention to volume and shadow, and a treatment closer to classical Western portraiture. The divergence between the two pieces — executed simultaneously and in the same medium — constitutes an involuntary document of each artist's pictorial sensibilities.

El Pupi · Technical Pen
[5]

Captain Prick Von Popplewell, known as "El Pupi"

Franco Salazar Date unknown Technical pen on paper Yalán-Salazar Collection

An informal piece incorporated into the present exhibition as an unannounced element. The subject — a domestic feline and permanent member of the Yalán-Salazar household — appears here accompanied by Señor Platanito, a personal play object of his daily use. The work was not produced in the context of the main session; its inclusion responds to criteria distinct from the rest of the collection, without diminishing its documentary value.

III Archive of Recent Years (2023–2026)
Archive · 2023–2026
[6]

Archive of Recent Years (2023–2026)

Yalán-Salazar · Joint production 2023–2026 3 Instax Mini photographs · 1 analogue strip (Manchester, 2025) Glass jar with cork stopper · Domestic feline hair Yalán-Salazar Collection

The ensemble documents four years of shared life through a selection of photographic objects of varying nature and provenance: an image of Captain Prick Von Popplewell; one of the last photographic records before the collectors' departure to the United Kingdom (2023); a portrait of the collectors on their tenth anniversary in Scotland (2024); an analogue photo-booth strip produced in Manchester (2025); and a photograph from the Gorillaz concert — the last photographic print made before the wedding (2026). The jar included in the composition contains hair from the feline subject of the collection, incorporated as a material representation of an attendant who, for logistical reasons, will be unable to be present at the ceremony.

IV From Families of Origin to Our Own
[7–9]

From Families of Origin to Our Own Family: Photographic Triptych

Various authors 2000–2022 · 3 framed printed photographs Yalán-Salazar Collection

The ensemble articulates, in three images, the biographical trajectory of the collection's founders: two childhood portraits taken within their respective families of origin, and a later portrait documenting the family they built together. The arrangement — with the childhood portraits in the upper position and the joint portrait below — inverts the usual genealogical convention and proposes a reading of convergence rather than descent.

V Projected Archive: Three Collections
Projected Video · Loop · approx. 5 min
[10]

Projected Archive: Three Collections

Jamilet Yalán · Compilation and editing Additional photography: Fernando Roncal (Lima) c. 1960s – 2026 · Looped video · With audio Approx. 5 min · Projected on fabric Yalán-Salazar Collection

The piece brings together three collections of different provenance and nature in a single continuous projection: records from the pre-wedding session held in Lima by photographer Fernando Roncal; photographs of the co-founder's grandmothers and mother on their respective wedding days; and an archive of founding moments in the collectors' shared life — including the signing of their first and second apartment and their engagement. The looped projection on fabric proposes a reading with no beginning or end, in which three generations of women and an ongoing story coexist simultaneously.
